Overview

What is Tiny Mentors?

Tiny Mentors is a kids learning app I designed and built from scratch — from the first Figma wireframe to a signed Android app on Google Play. It targets children aged 3–6, with six mini-games covering animals, letters, numbers, shapes, and memory. Sessions are short by design: each game is exactly 5 questions, then done.

The goal was to build something a parent would feel comfortable handing to a toddler: no ads, no in-app purchases, no endless scroll. Just focused, rewarding play in bite-sized chunks.

The Problem

Why another learning app?

Most kids apps fall into one of two traps: they are either overloaded with ads and dark-pattern loops designed to maximise screen time, or they feel like digital homework — sterile, joyless, and quickly abandoned. Parents I spoke to wanted something in between: genuinely educational, but actually fun.

The insight that shaped everything: young children learn best through repetition with variety and immediate reward. Five questions, a celebration at the end, then a natural stopping point. No lives, no timers, no pressure.

Design Decisions

What drove the key choices

🦔

Mascot system

Four animal mascots — Pip, Luna, Ziggy, and Sunny — each with a distinct personality. The child picks one at the start. It travels with them across every game, building familiarity and attachment rather than a blank generic interface.

🎨

Warm palette

Cream base (#fff6e5) with grape purple, berry pink, tangerine, and sun yellow accents. Deliberately softer than the typical saturated "kids app" look — warm enough to feel friendly, not so bright it causes eye strain.

🗺️

Map hub

Games are accessed from a visual adventure map, not a list. Navigation feels like choosing where to go next rather than selecting a menu item — a small change that makes the whole experience feel more alive.

⭐

Structured progression

Every game is exactly 5 questions with progress dots at the top. A star-based result screen at the end provides closure and celebration. No infinite loops, no lives lost — just a clear start and a satisfying finish.

The Games

Six mini-games

Each game targets a different learning area. The question format varies per game — multiple choice, tap-to-select, matching — so the interaction never gets repetitive even across sessions.

🐾 Animals

Identify animals from illustrations and audio prompts. The first game designed — sets the template for all the others.

🔤 Letters

Recognise uppercase and lowercase letters. Audio plays the letter sound; the child taps the matching card.

🔢 Numbers

Count objects and identify numbers 1–10. Visual grouping reinforces the concept before the label.

🔷 Shapes

Match shapes by name and visual form. Includes circle, square, triangle, rectangle, star, and more.

🧠 Memory

A classic card-flip matching game. Trains short-term memory and visual recognition in a familiar format.

🌱 Growing Garden

A nurturing game where the child plants and grows flowers. Introduces patience and sequential steps through play.

Technical

How it was built and shipped

The entire app is plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — no framework, no build system. This kept the bundle small, fast, and easy to iterate on. Game logic, animations, mascot theming, and audio are all vanilla JS.

To ship as a native Android app, I used Capacitor 8 to wrap the web app in a WebView and generate a signed Android App Bundle (AAB). The final build came in under 25 MB and was submitted to Google Play.

Development was done with Claude Code as an AI coding partner — handling everything from game logic and layout implementation to Android packaging and icon generation. It made solo development at this scope genuinely feasible.
