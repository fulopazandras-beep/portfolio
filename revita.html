Overview

What is ReVita?

ReVita is a mobile health app concept designed to bridge the gap between physiotherapy clinic sessions and home recovery. Patients receive personalised exercise programs, track their progress, and can message their therapist directly — all in one place.

The project covers the full design process: from early research and lo-fidelity sketches through mid-fidelity wireframes to a fully interactive hi-fi Figma prototype.

The Problem

Why patients don't follow through

A recurring issue in physiotherapy: patients do their exercises consistently during supervised sessions, then stop as soon as they go home. Without structure, feedback, or accountability, adherence drops dramatically between visits.

The design challenge was to recreate enough of that in-clinic feeling digitally — clear guidance, visible progress, and a direct line to the therapist — without overwhelming a non-tech-savvy user with a complex interface.

Design Decisions

What shaped the key choices

Dark, calm interface

A dark theme with soft purple and teal accents keeps the experience calm and clinical — deliberately avoiding the bright, gamified look of generic fitness apps. Recovery is serious; the UI reflects that.

Exercise dashboard first

The home screen leads with today's exercise program — not stats, not a feed. The most important action is always one tap away, reducing friction for users who just want to get their session done.

Expert chat built in

Direct messaging with the assigned therapist is a core feature, not an afterthought. Patients often have questions between visits; a low-friction channel reduces anxiety and improves outcomes.

Progress visibility

Recovery is slow and easy to give up on. Visible week-by-week progress — even small milestones — reinforces that the effort is working, keeping motivation up over longer treatment periods.

Process

Lo-fi → Mid-fi → Hi-fi

The design went through three distinct fidelity stages. Lo-fi sketches established the core flow and screen hierarchy. Mid-fi wireframes validated layout and navigation before committing to visual styling. The hi-fi prototype brought it all together with polished components, spacing, and motion — ready for user testing.
